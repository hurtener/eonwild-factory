## Close-out of the three former P1s

### Former P1-VIS-01 — explosive one-footed jump / airborne bite

**Disposition: CLOSED.**

The 24 fps render changes the evidence materially. In both views, frames approximately `n=48–54` (2.00–2.25 s) show the low back/down coil. Around `n=54–56` (2.25–2.33 s), the attack jaw opens while the load is still being released. In the three-quarter view, `n=58–64` (2.42–2.67 s) clearly shows both hind feet folded and separated above the floor while the jaws remain open; the side view corroborates the upward root/body arc and leg fold, with one limb partially occluded by the profile.

The red target proxy is visible ahead of the muzzle during the release, so the gape is no longer an unexplained pose pulse. The flight remains short, as in the reference, but it is now legible at delivery rate: a viewer can identify the launch, a brief airborne bite, and the descent. This closes the prior 10 fps sampling objection and the prior “no target / no readable flight” objection.

### Former P1-VIS-02 — walk-to-power support transfer / preload

**Disposition: CLOSED.**

The transition is now shorter and visually ordered. Frames approximately `n=24–44` (1.00–1.83 s) move from the in-place walk into a lower, target-oriented posture. Frames `n=46–54` (1.92–2.25 s) show the pelvis/trunk lowered, the near hind foot carrying the lower support line, and the opposite hind limb higher/folding rather than trailing as a disconnected rigid bone. The head lowers after the body begins the coil, then the jaw opens at the release.

The side profile alone still hides one limb at points, but the three-quarter sheet makes the asymmetric load readable: one hind foot is the support/launch base and the other is unweighted before toe-off. This is sufficient visual evidence for the requested one-foot preload; the remaining subtlety is a P2 polish opportunity, not a P1 failure.

### Former P1-VIS-03 — lead landing, weight arrest, and target feeding handoff

**Disposition: CLOSED.**

Frames approximately `n=64–76` (2.67–3.17 s) show the descent into the first catch. The lead foot reaches down first; the opposite foot remains later/folded and then comes down over the following frames. The three-quarter view is especially useful here because it separates the two hind feet.

Frames approximately `n=80–100` (3.33–4.17 s) hold a stable, braced post-catch body before the feeding transition. This is a restrained arrest rather than a dramatic squash, but it reads as a stop/settle. Frames `n=104–148` (4.33–6.17 s) lower the head toward the fixed target position, and frames after that continue the head-down feeding loop with visible jaw pulses. The target proxy remains in the review shot and the muzzle is directed toward it, so the earlier “targetless head-down animation” finding is closed for this review composition.

The proxy is intentionally an evidence marker, not a claim that a prey mesh is already in the runtime GLB. That boundary is recorded as P2 below.

## Jaw and full-body checks

### Jaw centering

**PASS.** The three-quarter attack frames show the upper and lower jaws opening around the same cranial centerline without a visible lateral hinge drift. The jaw review contact sheet also shows no obvious off-axis opening. The release validation reports maximum off-hinge rotation of approximately `1.28e-14°` and a maximum skull-space midline error of `0.001526 m` against a `0.002 m` gate; this agrees with the visual result.

### Full-body framing and texture

**PASS.** Head, feet, torso, and tail remain inside both final film frames. There is no visible disappearance, catastrophic mesh collapse, or missing-material fallback. The pale scale texture remains readable throughout the final power films. The dark stage provides enough contrast for the feet and target, although a future editorial pass could increase contact-shadow readability.

### Leg chain and toes

**PASS with minor polish debt.** The near hind leg compresses, releases, folds, and reaches in a connected chain. The three-quarter view makes the staggered foot positions readable and shows articulated toes rather than a rigid flat sole. Exact individual digit angles are not scientifically calibrated by this review; the conclusion is visual coherence against the supplied source choreography.

## Remaining P2 follow-up items

These do not block the final visual P1 gate:

1. **Review proxy boundary.** The red target is a project-owned abstract Blender-only proxy and is not embedded in the GLB. A runtime interaction shot with the real game prey/carcass asset remains future work.
2. **Arrest amplitude.** The landing-to-arrest phase is readable as a stable brace, but the body compression is deliberately subtle. If the page needs a stronger impact beat, a later pass could add a small, source-consistent compression without reopening the core attack choreography.
3. **Presentation consistency.** The final power films use the locked dark stage and pale texture look. If they are compared beside older idle/walk clips, standardize the stage, exposure, and color management so the material difference is not mistaken for an animation difference.
4. **Mirrored visual sample.** The release validation covers both mirrored action events, but this review directly inspected the supplied side and three-quarter films for one presentation. A dedicated mirrored film would strengthen visual symmetry evidence; no P1 is inferred from its absence.
