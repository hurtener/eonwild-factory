## Measured gap

B has the intended lateral timing. At the two single-support midpoints it
places the pelvis ±40.87 mm laterally, rolls it ±1.50°, and reaches the high
point of the existing stance-vault height carrier. At the double-support
midpoints it is 36.33 mm lower and nearly centered/level. Those extrema occur
near 0.765/1.995 s (single support) and 0.150/1.380 s (double support).

The remaining side-profile problem is concrete: no authored sagittal carrier
exists. Pelvis pitch about the declared lateral axis spans only 0.0262° and
chest pitch 0.0377° over the 2.46 s cycle. The neck and head hold a nearly
constant downward attention posture: their pitch excursions are 0.0253–0.0350°
within offsets of about −0.36° to −2.99°. Tail base/tip pitch excursions are
only 0.134°/0.338° and are inherited from other world rotations; the authored
tail control is yaw-only. These are not a visible, support-timed body-pitch
response.

`sample_grounded_gait` emits linear root travel (`root_forward_m = velocity ×
time`), which is 1.10764 m/s for B. It supplies a vertical stance-vault carrier,
but no fore/aft body carrier or velocity change. `apply_performance` supplies
support-timed lateral sway/roll, global-sine pelvis yaw, tail yaw, and a
gaze-elevation correction; it has no support-timed pelvis/chest/neck/head/tail
pitch control. The yaw sine is also not support-locked: its quarter-cycle peaks
at 0.615 and 1.845 s, before the 0.763 and 1.993 s stance midpoints, and it is
still about ±0.746° at double-support midpoints.

This explains the host result: B improves lateral transfer without creating a
sagittal load-and-release silhouette, so the side and forward-moving profiles
remain level and even. Constant root speed is appropriate for this steady gait,
but it contributes no fore/aft phase cue by itself.

Rubenson et al. (2004, doi:10.1098/rspb.2004.2702) distinguishes pendular
walking from grounded running in ostriches; it does not provide a Tarbosaurus
bounce amplitude to copy. Bishop et al. (2021, doi:10.1126/sciadv.abi7348)
discusses tail inertia in Coelophysis, not a measured Tarbosaurus response.
They support a qualitative phase-coherence question only, not numerical
Tarbosaurus targets.
